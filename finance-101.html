Learn · do · review

The four stages of financial fluency.

1
Foundation · Know your numbers

Build your personal balance sheet

Financial literacy begins with seeing what is true today—not judging yourself for it. Track a full month of money in and money out, then take a snapshot of what you own and owe.

  • Separate gross salary from take-home pay; note irregular income like commission, bonuses, or grants.
  • List assets at realistic resale value and liabilities at current settlement value.
  • Calculate net worth (assets minus liabilities) and monthly savings rate. A negative number is a starting point, not a verdict.
Field assignment

Review 90 days of statements. Categorise every transaction. Write down one surprising pattern and one change you can sustain for 30 days.

2
Stability · Protect your downside

Make the plan hard to break

Resilience is a wealth strategy. Before chasing returns, make sure a car repair, income interruption, or family emergency will not force you into costly debt or a rushed sale.

  • Start a liquid emergency reserve sized to your responsibilities and income stability; build it progressively.
  • Prioritise high-cost debt while maintaining required payments and checking fees and early-settlement terms.
  • Review medical, life, disability, and household cover against actual needs; avoid paying for duplicate or unsuitable cover.
Field assignment

Price three likely emergencies. Set a first reserve milestone and automate an affordable transfer on payday. Compare one debt’s effective annual cost with a realistic payoff plan.

3
Growth · Invest with a thesis

Choose a system, not a hot tip

Investing means accepting uncertainty in exchange for a possible future return. Match each investment to a goal, time horizon, need for access, risk capacity, and total cost.

  • Learn the difference between an individual share, a bond, a pooled fund, an index fund, and an ETF.
  • Compare broad diversification, fund fees, tax treatment, currency exposure, and the ability to stay invested through a downturn.
  • Model a regular contribution over time; test lower returns, inflation, fees, and missed contributions—not only the best case.
Field assignment

Compare two diversified funds using their factsheets. Record benchmark, holdings, total fees, domicile, risks, and tax questions. Explain your choice without mentioning last year’s performance.

4
Mastery · Govern and adapt

Build a portfolio you can live with

Strategic wealth architecture is a set of connected decisions—not a guarantee. A sound plan makes room for taxes, family obligations, liquidity, concentration, behaviour, and uncertainty.

  • Write a one-page investment policy: goals, allocation range, contribution rule, rebalancing rule, and reasons to change course.
  • Stress-test job loss, market falls, higher rates, a currency move, and a delayed property sale. Identify what you would do before it happens.
  • Treat leverage and “arbitrage” claims as risk questions: confirm financing costs, taxes, liquidity, counterparty risk, and exit conditions.
Mastery simulation

Draft a household plan for a 20% market fall and six months without earned income. Define cash available, obligations due, and decisions you will postpone.

Uncommon resources & useful shortcuts

Better questions beat secret tricks.

Use primary sources and slow down whenever a product is hard to explain. The shortest route is usually a clear process, not a higher-risk bet.

Read the source documentFund factsheets, fee schedules, loan agreements, tax certificates, and official benefit rules beat marketing summaries.
Use a one-page money mapList accounts, beneficiaries, policies, debts, renewal dates, and a trusted contact. Keep it private and up to date.
Automate the boring partSchedule an affordable saving or investment contribution after payday; review annually and when life changes.
Find independent helpCheck adviser credentials and how they are paid. Ask what is excluded, what it costs, and whether advice is regulated in your jurisdiction.
Use a cooling-off ruleFor an unfamiliar investment, write down the thesis, worst case, fees, and exit conditions; wait before committing.
South African starting pointsSARS guidance, FSCA consumer education, National Treasury, and product-provider disclosures. Rules and thresholds change—check current official sources.
